## Why the v3 policy does not move

Diagnosed to the frame, and the diagnosis was wrong twice before it was right —
both wrong versions are recorded in yesterday's file so they are not re-derived.

Every take begins with the operator holding the arm still at the reference pose:
median 1.43 s, **~10% of all frames**, and through them `action[t] - state[t]` is
**exactly 0.000**. The dataset's single loudest statement is "at this exact
observation, stay where you are."

The old `glassbottle_pick` carries the same dead time (9.7%) and its policy moved
— because its start pose varied by 18.5 units, smearing those frames across
twenty states. `v3` replays the pose to 0.3 units, so all of it lands on one.

ACT is Markovian on (image, state) with **no clock**. It cannot know it has
already been still for thirty seconds. Measured directly, six consecutive
inferences from the start pose:

```
-105.66  -105.74  -105.80  -105.72  -105.80  -105.85
```

0.19 units of variation — a fixed point. And `action_lift` never goes below
-100 in any of 8 621 training frames, so -105.66 is an overshoot below a floor
the model was taught to sit on. The arm is already at its lowest, so nothing
happens, the observation never changes, and the same command comes back forever.

**Pinning the start pose was necessary to force visual conditioning. It also
built this. Both are true.**

## What the trim does

`scripts/trim_start_dead_frames.py` cuts on the **commanded step**
`|action[t] - state[t]|`, not on distance travelled. The first version cut on
travel and was wrong: the frame it kept commanded 0.09 units, roughly one raw
count, inside the servo deadband — the deadlock survives a frame longer.

Two requirements pull opposite ways and both hold at threshold 1.0:

| | |
|---|---|
| frame 0's command must move a servo | **≥ 1.08 units (~13 raw counts)** |
| frame 0 must resemble the replayed pose | **≤ 2.53 units drift** |

They fit only because the arm has barely moved when the command grows — the
servo lags the operator's push. Do not raise the threshold without re-checking
the second column.

Video is never re-encoded: frames are addressed by `from_timestamp + timestamp`,
so trimming rebases timestamps and pushes `from_timestamp` forward, and dropped
frames stop being referenced. All four mp4s are byte-identical afterwards. Image
statistics are deliberately not recomputed — training sets
`use_imagenet_stats: True`, so they are never read.

## What is still unknown

The operator taped a line on the table to bound the camera's field of view. It

```
                 tape x     drift        shoulder_pan equivalent
recorded          602.3      --                --
found today      >=635      >=+33 px         >=+2.5 units
after realigning  600.7        -1.6 px         -0.14 units
```

For scale, the policy's own learned pan bias is 0.9 units, so the drifted rig
outweighed it roughly threefold. The mechanism is direct: the bottle appeared
~30 px right of where that physical spot appeared in training, so the policy drove
to where a bottle at that image position used to be — right of the real one.

`scripts/check_scene.py` reports it, and `camera_preview.py --align` tracks it
live with the target drawn, which is the practical way to put the table back.

**What this cannot distinguish:** a rotated base moves camera and gripper
together and largely cancels; a moved table does not. Both look identical here.

## Two traps, both hit today

**A clipped band has no usable centroid.** When the tape touches the frame edge
the bright run is cut off and its centroid is pulled inward, reading as *less*
drift than there is. The +33.1 px measured that way is a lower bound, not a
measurement. Both tools now return "off frame" rather than a flattering number.

**Do not measure the fiducial on the annotated stream.** `camera_preview --align`
burns the target and tracking lines into the JPEG. A frame pulled from
`/stream.mjpg` and re-analysed measures those lines as well as the tape — it read
+4.5 px against the overlay's own -1.6 px. The overlay computes on the clean
frame before drawing; that is the number to trust. Pull frames for *looking*, not
for measuring.

```
tape realigned to -1.6 px   lighting NOT restored (flat, warm)
```

From `/tmp/trial.csv` (`--log-trajectory`, added today): commanded gripper reached
**7.68**, state stopped at **12.89** — the gap is the bottle blocking the jaws.
Closed from step 554 to the end. A real grasp, not a lucky final pose.

## Four errors, found and fixed in this order

| | size | fix |
|---|---|---|
| chunk horizon truncating the plan | total failure | `--n-action-steps` 10 -> 50/100 |
| rig geometry drift | >=2.5 pan units | realign tape to x=602 |
| learned pan bias | 0.9 units | `--aim-offset shoulder_pan=-0.9` |
| lighting | unquantified | STILL NOT FIXED |

Feedback cannot remove a constant bias in the perception-to-action map — it
reappears every step. Lowering `--n-action-steps` was the wrong lever for the aim
and cost the grasp (below); the offset is what fixed it.

## The dwell pathology appears twice, not once

The arm reached the bottle in 6 s, matching the demos, then **hovered for 14 s**
bumping it before closing — 9 direction reversals in `shoulder_lift` against a
demo mean of 1.0, and 19.9 s to grasp against a demo mean of 9.5 s.

The cause is the same as the opening freeze, at a different point:

```
dwell at the bottle before the gripper closes, per demo
  80  88  25  70 114  67  77 109  74  75  94  95  76  73  97  76  85 117 113  83
  mean 84 frames (2.8 s)   max 117
```

At `--n-action-steps 50` the close command sits beyond the execution window and is
never reached; the runner re-executes the first 50 steps of "hover, then close"
indefinitely. It escaped on accumulated drift, not because the plan completed.

**So the horizon must exceed the dwell, wherever the dwell falls.** With the bias
now corrected by offset rather than by feedback, 100 is affordable again.

## For the retrain: trim BOTH dwells, but not identically

`trim_start_dead_frames.py` only addresses the start. The pre-grasp dwell causes
the same stall and is three times longer.

They are not the same thing, though. The start dead-time is operator reaction lag
and safe to remove entirely. The pre-grasp dwell is plausibly **functional** — the
operator settling and confirming alignment before committing the jaws. Removing it
risks a policy that closes early on nothing. Shorten it to ~30 frames rather than
cutting it, and treat that as an experiment with a control.

Still true: `v4` cannot be trained as built, its gripper never opening past 60.4.

## What this success is and is not

It is proof the pipeline works end to end. It is **not** a measurement. Lighting
was flat and warm against a side-lit neutral reference, an `--aim-offset` was
applied, and n=1. Block A still needs restored lighting, a re-derived offset under
that lighting, and the horizon settled.
