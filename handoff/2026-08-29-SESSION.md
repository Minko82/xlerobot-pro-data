## The grasp diagnosis — read this before recording anything

The policy reproduces the demonstrations almost exactly and still misses the
bottle on hardware. Three offline measurements, no robot needed, explain why.

**It reproduces the demonstrations.** Per-joint error against the human is
1.5–6.4 % of each joint's demonstrated range, and the gripper closes within
0.10 s of where the human closed it, slightly tighter. The grasp behaviour is
learned. Nothing is wrong with the training.

**It leans on proprioception, not vision.** Swapping the camera image for a
different episode's, leaving joint state untouched, moves the prediction only
7.4 units against an across-episode spread of 21.3 — vision accounts for about
35 % of the variation the policy has to explain. On the robot, joint state
cannot tell you where the bottle is.

**Because the demonstrations never asked it to look.** The bottle sat on a fixed
mark in all 20 takes while the *arm* start pose varied:

```
start-pose spread across the 20 episodes
  wrist_flex      58.0 units   (9.1 -> 67.1)
  shoulder_pan    20.5
  shoulder_lift   10.3
  mean arm spread 18.5 units      (task-scale variation is ~21)
```

That is backwards. The thing that varied was the thing proprioception can see,
and the thing that stayed fixed was the thing only the camera can see. A
state-conditioned trajectory replayer fits that data perfectly, and that is what
you trained. On hardware it starts from a pose it has not quite seen, reaches
where its joints say rather than where the bottle is, and misses confidently.

**The fix is two changes to the recording protocol, not more of the same data:**

- **Fix the start pose.** `AUTONOMY_PROTOCOL.md` §1 already requires it (*"arms
  at the reference pose"*), and `calibration/hold_pose.json` is the replayed
  reference the thermal sweep uses for exactly this reason. Drive to it before
  each take instead of starting wherever the arm was left.
- **Vary the bottle instead.** If the bottle moves and the start pose does not,
  proprioception *cannot* solve the task and vision has to carry it.

Recording 20 more takes without these changes will bake in the same weakness.

```bash
  --task "pick up the glass bottle from the shelf"'
```

**There is no "press ENTER when clear" prompt — the arm moves immediately.**
Clear the workspace first. Ctrl-C now shortens the torque bleed rather than
skipping it.

`--n-action-steps 10` is the setting to keep. ACT queues `n_action_steps`
actions and re-infers only when the queue empties, so the trained default of 50
commits 1.7 s of open-loop motion and the correction on refill is visible as
twitchiness. 10 is observably smoother:

| | realised rate | peak joint |
|---|---|---|
| `n_action_steps=50` | 28.21 Hz | 59 °C after 30 s |
| `n_action_steps=10` | 23.89 Hz | 35 °C after 13.7 s |

**Do not quote the runner's `inference 9.6 ms median` in the paper.** It is not
the model's forward cost. ACT only invokes the model when its queue empties, so
with K=50 just ~17 of 847 steps ran it; the median describes the 830 that did
not. The real per-call cost is in the loop overrun: ~105 ms at K=50 and ~85 ms
at K=10, which agree well enough to trust. That also rules out temporal
ensembling — it needs inference every step, so ~100 ms caps it near 10 Hz.

## The bus fault — measured, mitigated, not repaired

Four sessions died today to a single class of fault before it was characterised.

```
sync_read of all six    3 / 2399     0.125 %
individual reads        5 / 14 394   0.035 %      3.6x better
ids 2, 3, 4 dropped; ids 1, 5, 6 stayed clean
```

Failures are **not a clean suffix of the daisy chain**, so this is not one bad
cable, and there is no reason to replace the elbow lead that was changed on the
27th. The broadcast read failing 3.6× more often than the same six motors read
individually is marginal timing on the shared half-duplex line.

At 0.125 % per call, a 25 s take is ~750 broadcast reads — about one expected
failure per episode, and lerobot retries **zero** times. That is why sessions
kept ending. `harden_bus()` in both `record_kinesthetic.py` and
`act_policy_control.py` now retries six times with backoff doubling from 10 ms
(~310 ms window). Three tries at a flat 10 ms was **not** enough; calibration
still died with `retrying` visible in the traceback.

This is mitigation. `scripts/bus_watch.py --limp` reproduces and localises it —
run it with the arm limp and moving, because a stationary arm reads clean.

`wrist_roll` was left unfixed deliberately: it travels 0.0–3.0 units across all

**The archived `medbottle_pick_*` episodes were recorded on the old calibration
and cannot be mixed with anything recorded now.** They are a record of what
happened, not training data.

If a calibration ever aborts partway, the motors keep the new homing offsets
while the file keeps the old ones, `is_calibrated` goes False and recording
refuses to start. Recovery: run `--calibrate` and press **plain ENTER** (not
`c`) — that writes the file's values back to the motors.

## Open questions for tomorrow

**Does the start-pose fix actually recover the grasp?** The diagnosis is
evidence-backed but the fix is untested. If a fixed start pose plus a varying
bottle does not raise the grasp rate, the next suspect is dataset size — 20 is
ACT's floor and 40–50 is where single-task reliability usually arrives.

**Is 59 °C after 30 s reproducible?** n=1, and the starting temperature was not
recorded. If real, it matters a great deal: Block A is 30 back-to-back trials
against a 65 °C abort ceiling, and you would hit it within a handful. That is
not a problem, it is the result your protocol says nobody else reports — but
record the trial-start temperature from trial 1 and run `log_thermal_power.py`
across the whole block, not per trial.

**Do not start the trial block until the policy grasps sometimes.** Block A
measures success *as a function of* temperature. A 0/20 success rate has no
decay curve in it, only `GRASP` codes.
