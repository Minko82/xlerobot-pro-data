## TL;DR — the policy reads the operator's hand

**Kinesthetic teaching left the operator's hand on the wrist, inside the wrist
camera's view, in every approach and grasp frame of every episode.** Checkpoint
040000 learned to use it. At deployment the hand is absent, and the policy
plans the grasp ~25 units of `shoulder_lift` short and closes ~40 steps early.
It looks exactly like an aim bias and it is not one.

Evidence (`scripts/hand_overlay_test.py`, all on checkpoint 040000):

```
frame                                              planned close   lift  elbow
trial 7 step 120, as the camera saw it                  step 47    25.2   60.2
  + hand region pasted from training ep13 g-120         never      56.7   45.7
  + hand region pasted from training ep13 g-60          step 83    55.1   55.8
training ep13 g-60, as recorded (truth 57 / 57)         step 61    58.5   53.4
training ep13 g-60, hand region replaced by trial 7     step 42    41.8   53.3
operator's grasp for that bottle position (regression)             +49    +30
```

Paste a hand in and the plan moves to the operator's pose. Take the hand out of
a training frame and the plan goes short and early — the deployment failure,
reproduced offline.

This is why `measure_bias.py` reported only +1.6 / -1.6 on training frames
(the hand is present there) while live trials missed by 25–30 units. Any
offline bias measurement on kinesthetic data has this blind spot.

## For the paper

Kinesthetic demonstration puts the demonstrator in the observation. A wrist
camera makes that unavoidable, and a 49-episode ACT policy will learn the
demonstrator rather than the scene. That is a result, and the masked retrain is
the controlled test of it: same data, same steps, hand painted out.
