## TL;DR — the policy reads the operator's hand

**Kinesthetic teaching left the operator's hand on the wrist, inside the wrist
camera's view, in every approach and grasp frame of every episode.** Checkpoint
040000 learned to use it. At deployment the hand is absent, and the policy
plans the grasp ~25 units of `shoulder_lift` short and closes ~40 steps early.
It looks exactly like an aim bias and it is not one.

Evidence (`scripts/hand_overlay_test.py`, all on checkpoint 040000):

```
frame                                              planned close   lift  elbow
trial 7 step 120, as the camera saw it                  step 47    25.2   60.2
  + hand region pasted from training ep13 g-120         never      56.7   45.7
  + hand region pasted from training ep13 g-60          step 83    55.1   55.8
training ep13 g-60, as recorded (truth 57 / 57)         step 61    58.5   53.4
training ep13 g-60, hand region replaced by trial 7     step 42    41.8   53.3
operator's grasp for that bottle position (regression)             +49    +30
```

Paste a hand in and the plan moves to the operator's pose. Take the hand out of
a training frame and the plan goes short and early — the deployment failure,
reproduced offline.

This is why `measure_bias.py` reported only +1.6 / -1.6 on training frames
(the hand is present there) while live trials missed by 25–30 units. Any
offline bias measurement on kinesthetic data has this blind spot.

## Findings from the code-only tests, ~11 pm (v7 checkpoint 010000, v6 040000)

Full tables: `trials/eval_v7_010000.md`; figures: `trials/figures/`.

1. **The mask works on training frames.** v7 at 10k: pan intercept +0.19,
   mean error -0.04 (v6 had +0.57 at 40k); start-frame motion onset 56 vs 56
   recorded; close timing from g-60/g-30 within 1–5 frames of the operator.
2. **Both policies read the bottle.** Image-swap ablation: same joint state,
   substitute a training start frame with the bottle on the right, and the
   planned pan goes from -29 to +25; a far bottle raises the planned lift to +39
   and straightens the elbow to -37. Vision is not being ignored.
3. **The live short close is a depth ambiguity plus an open-loop horizon.**
   From trial 7 step 120, v6 and v7-10k both plan a close ~50 steps out at lift
   +20–25 (operator: +49). The training frames whose joint state matches that
   moment come from episode 40, whose bottle sat next to the robot — and at
   that arm state its bottle lands on the same image pixels as trial 7's
   (figure `trial7_step120_vs_state_matched_training_frames.png`). A single
   wrist RGB camera cannot separate the two by depth; the operator separated
   them by continuing to move until the bottle was between the jaws. The policy
   instead executes its 100-step chunk open-loop (3.3 s) and closes where the
   plan said, with the bottle still above the jaws (trial 7 frame 150).
4. **Plans are not time-compressed on training frames**: shoulder_lift lead
   over the chunk +1.7 (v6) / -0.8 (v7-10k) units; where the plan closes
   within the chunk it does so at the operator's frame (63 vs 63/67).

So the remaining fault is deployment-side: `--n-action-steps 100` was chosen
to survive the start-pose dwell, and it costs all visual correction during the
reach. Two candidates, both testable tomorrow, neither needing a retrain:

- **Temporal ensembling** (ACT's own `temporal_ensemble_coeff`, e.g. 0.01):
  infer every step, execute the exponentially weighted average of every chunk's
  prediction for the current step. Breaks the start-pose deadlock (chunks made
  earlier predict motion for now) and corrects the reach every 33 ms. Runner
  flag added: `--temporal-ensemble COEFF` (forces n_action_steps 1).
  `scripts/ensemble_sim.py`: fed the unchanged start observation every step,
  the ensembled lift command begins rising at step 73 (0.01) / 65 (0.05) for
  both v6 and v7 — the dwell does not lock. Runner path verified in code
  against 040000 (no robot): action shape (1, 6), lift rising from step ~65.
  A full inference every step costs 47 ms, so the loop runs ~21 Hz and the arm
  ~30 % slower than the demos. Acceptable for a first trial. Try 0.01 first.
- **Adaptive horizon**: 100 steps until shoulder_lift has moved, then 30–40.

## For the paper

Kinesthetic demonstration puts the demonstrator in the observation. A wrist
camera makes that unavoidable, and a 49-episode ACT policy will learn the
demonstrator rather than the scene. That is a result, and the masked retrain is
the controlled test of it: same data, same steps, hand painted out.
