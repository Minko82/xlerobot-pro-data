## Fixes made tonight, in this repo, NOT yet on the robot

`scripts/record_kinesthetic.py` — `neck_position()` now returns None when the
arm is running on `/dev/xle_head`.

Two reasons, both of which bit during the session:

- The neck sits at IDs 1-2 on the head bus, which are also the arm's
  `shoulder_pan` and `shoulder_lift`. With the arm on that port it was reading
  the ARM and reporting shoulder movement between takes as camera drift. The
  `CAMERA MOVED 94 counts` warnings you saw were false. **The camera did not
  move. Do not restart the dataset over them.**
- It opened a SECOND handle on a port the robot already held, once per saved
  episode. Two handles interleaving on one half-duplex bus corrupts traffic, and
  that is the most likely cause of both mid-session failures:
  `Failed to write 'Lock' on id_=5` at connect, and
  `Failed to sync read 'Present_Position' on ids=[1..6]` in episode 3.

An earlier fix in the same file IS already on the robot: image arrays are copied
out of librealsense's 16-deep frame pool before being retained, which is what
was killing every run at 14-15 frames.

## Open questions for tomorrow

**Did the arm actually move in episodes 1 and 2?** The console `MOVING` /
`still` indicator cannot answer this from the log. It redraws in place with
`\r`, so the only lines that survive in scrollback are the instants when a
gripper message forced a newline — and those are exactly the moments your hands
were on the keyboard rather than the arm. `still` at those instants is what a
good take looks like. Measure joint travel in the saved data instead.

**Lighting.** The plan is to vary it across takes. Worth a decision rather than
a default: varying lighting buys robustness, but at 20 episodes the policy sees
each condition only a handful of times, and with a dataset this small that
usually costs more than it buys. The safer version is to keep lighting fixed for
the 20 that train the policy, then vary it deliberately at TEST time to measure
how far robustness actually extends. That gives a result to report rather than a
confound to explain.

## Power: shut down, do not cut

Hard-cutting power is what put the ext4 errors on this card. ext4's journal
protects metadata, not data, and underneath it the SD card's flash translation
layer rewrites its own block map constantly -- corrupt that and `fsck` cannot
see it, let alone fix it. Episodes also encode for 14-56 s AFTER the take ends;
a cut in that window can take the dataset's metadata with it, not just the take.

```bash
ssh xle@192.168.1.14 'sudo systemctl poweroff'    # wait for ping to stop, then cut
```

Cutting a genuinely hung machine is fine. Doing it routinely is the damage.

Worth checking tomorrow while it is up: `dmesg | grep -i ext4` for errors, and
whether the card has ever remounted read-only.
